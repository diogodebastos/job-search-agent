# Market note: Portugal

Habits to check, not laws. When a posting says otherwise, the posting wins. Add what you learn to `my/learnings.md`.

## Language
- Write in the posting's language. Many multinationals in Lisbon and Porto post in English; Portuguese retail, agencies and public bodies usually post in Portuguese.
- European Portuguese spelling and vocabulary (post-1990 orthographic agreement): "equipa", "gestão", "utilizador", "telemóvel". Not Brazilian forms ("time", "usuário", "celular").
- Formal but warm: "Exmos. Senhores" is dated for most companies; "Caro/a recrutador/a" or "Cara equipa de <empresa>" reads better. Close with "Com os melhores cumprimentos".
- State your language levels plainly (nativo, fluente, intermédio) or with the European framework (C1, B2) if you can back it up.

## CV habits
- One or two pages. A photo is common but not required; leave it out unless the user wants it or the posting asks.
- Don't include: citizen card or tax numbers, marital status, date of birth, full home address. City is enough.
- Europass is rarely asked for in the private sector; use it only when the posting says so.
- Degrees: give the Portuguese name the first time ("Licenciatura", "Mestrado") and, for an English CV, the usual equivalent (B.A./B.Sc., M.A./M.Sc.).

## Forms and questions you will meet
- "Pretensão salarial" (salary expectation) is asked often. Portugal usually talks **gross per month × 14 months**, sometimes annual gross. Ask the user for their floor in both forms and keep it in `preferences.json`. Answer with a range whose bottom is at or above the floor, or "negotiable, in line with the role" if the user prefers not to anchor.
- Meal allowance ("subsídio de alimentação") and other benefits come on top of base pay; compare offers on the whole package.
- "Disponibilidade" (availability): the legal notice period depends on tenure (commonly 30 or 60 days). Ask the user for theirs; never guess.
- Many large employers use an applicant system with its own form (re-typing the CV into fields). Prepare `form-answers.md` with each field ready to paste.

## Where roles are posted
- Company career pages first (they are the source; job boards copy them and go stale).
- LinkedIn Jobs, Net-Empregos, Indeed, Sapo Emprego; ITJobs and Landing.jobs for tech; agencies' own sites and Instagram/LinkedIn for marketing and creative roles.
- LinkedIn postings often need a login to read. If a link can't be fetched, ask the user to paste the text.

## Ways in
- The market is small and personal: a recommendation ("cunha" in its honest sense, someone vouching for you) carries real weight. University alumni groups, former colleagues, agency-client relationships and industry events are the usual routes.
- A short LinkedIn note in Portuguese to someone on the team, sent the day you apply, is normal.
