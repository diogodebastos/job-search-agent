# Learnings: what earlier job searches taught

These come from a real search run with this loop (about 20 applications over 3 years, mostly to one large tech company, plus a few others in 2026). They are observations from one person in one market, so treat them as starting assumptions and let `my/learnings.md` overrule them as your own evidence comes in.

Each entry is **what happened → what to do**.

## The way in matters more than the CV
- **L-01.** Across 15 applications to one company, exactly one reached an interview. It was the one where an employee had forwarded the hiring manager's own "I am hiring" post before the application went in. Cold applications: 12 sent, 0 conversations.
  → Before applying, spend ten minutes looking for a way in: someone on the team, the person who posted the job, an alumnus, a friend of a friend. Log which way in each application used.
- **L-02.** A high fit score did not rescue cold applications: three with fit 78 to 86 and full keyword coverage were rejected in 3 to 6 days, by template email, before any call.
  → A fast template rejection usually means a recruiter filter (title, seniority, pay), not a weak CV. Don't rewrite the CV after one; change the way in.
- **L-03.** A stranger who answers "please apply through the site" is not a warm contact.
  → Count it as outreach that didn't convert. Expect cold odds.
- **L-04.** Timing matters inside the warm channel: a contact who took the CV two weeks *after* the application changed nothing; a contact *before* it led to the interview.
  → Outreach comes before or on the day of applying.
- **L-05.** After a rejection, "can I send you my CV?" got a brush-off. A short note with no links and no job ask ("15 minutes for a coffee, three questions about your team's work") got a yes within a day.
  → Ask for a conversation about the work, not for a job. Make it small and easy to say yes to.
- **L-06.** Contacts move on. The one person who had opened doors left the company.
  → Keep more than one relationship alive, and check a contact still works there before counting on them.

## Choosing where to apply
- **L-07.** The best keyword match is not always the best role: one near-perfect match had an interview format that played to the candidate's weakest skill, and another paid well under their current salary.
  → Score the practical fit too: pay band against your floor, seniority, location, and what the interviews will test.
- **L-08.** A manager applying to a mid-level role with a pay band below their current pay was screened out in three days.
  → Skip roles whose top of band is under your floor. They cost an application and tend to read as "overqualified or too expensive".
- **L-09.** Re-applying to the same role title with the same CV never worked. The one repeat that did had a new way in.
  → Before re-applying, change something real: the way in first, then new evidence, or a different team.

## The CV and the letter
- **L-10.** An early draft inflated numbers ("100+ users" for 30). It was caught only because every number had to trace to a confirmed fact.
  → Every number comes from the facts file. If you can't defend it in an interview, it doesn't go in.
- **L-11.** The first tailored CV came out at 3 pages and took four tries to fit 2.
  → Plan the length from the first draft: about 80 lines for two pages. Current role 5 to 7 bullets, older roles 1 to 3.
- **L-12.** A CV with emoji in its headings and no email in its text was unreadable to résumé software.
  → Plain text, standard headings, contact details as text.
- **L-13.** A must-have the candidate truly lacks cannot be fixed by wording.
  → Name the gap in one honest line of the letter, with what you are doing about it. Never stuff the keyword.
- **L-14.** Gmail flagged a CV as "virus detected" because of links to free-hosting domains inside the PDF.
  → Email the PDFs to yourself before sending. Prefer links on your own domain or well-known sites.
- **L-15.** Restyling the CV and letter in the company's own look (colours, type, layout, original artwork in their style) was the thing reviewers remarked on. It takes a real study of their site, not a colour swap.
  → Use `/brand-cv` when a person, not just software, will open the files. Keep the plain version as a fallback, and check the styled one still parses.

## Interviews and after
- **L-16.** The one interview reached was lost, in the candidate's own reading, by leading with technical depth when the manager wanted proof of explaining results to non-specialists.
  → Prepare two stories about turning your work into a decision someone else made. Open with the outcome; keep the detail for when it is asked for.
- **L-17.** Reply times ranged from the same day to over 100 days; one in five never answered.
  → Don't call an application dead before about 6 weeks. Follow up once after 10 working days.
- **L-18.** Building the facts file took about 20 questions the first time, and nothing after: every later application reused it.
  → Do `/grill-cv` properly once. It is the most valuable hour of the whole search.
